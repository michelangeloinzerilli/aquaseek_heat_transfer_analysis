# Heat Transfer Optimization using SolidWorks Flow Simulation

Bachelor's Thesis developed at **Politecnico di Torino** in Mechanical Engineering, building on work started during a curricular internship at **Aquaseek S.R.L.**

## Overview

This project investigates the thermal behaviour of a **U-shaped heat exchanger** using **SolidWorks Flow Simulation**.

The heat exchanger consists of a copper tube surrounded by **alginate**, a biopolymer used within an atmospheric water harvesting technology developed by Aquaseek. The study focuses on modelling the heat transfer between flowing water, the copper structure, the alginate layer and the surrounding environment.

## Methodology

Several progressively refined simulations were developed to evaluate the temperature distribution within the system.

The analysis included:

- **Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD)** simulations of water flow and heat transfer
- Conjugate heat transfer between water, copper and alginate
- Comparison of different thermal boundary conditions
- Surface and volumetric heat-generation models
- Mesh sensitivity analysis
- Transient thermal simulations using experimentally derived heat-transfer data
- External airflow modelling around the heat exchanger
- Analysis of temperature distributions using sections, streamlines and exported simulation data

The simulations were based on the conservation of **mass, momentum and energy**, solved numerically within SolidWorks Flow Simulation.

## Repository Structure

- **SolidWorks model files** – CAD geometry of the U-shaped heat exchanger used in the simulations.
- **Steady-state simulation files** – SolidWorks Flow Simulation studies used to evaluate the thermal behaviour of the system under steady operating conditions.
- **Time-dependent simulation files** – Transient thermal simulation studies used to analyse the evolution of the alginate temperature over time.
- **Report** – Complete thesis describing the modelling assumptions, simulation configurations and thermal analyses.

The different simulation configurations progressively introduce more detailed representations of the heat-transfer process, including external heat exchange, volumetric thermal effects, transient behaviour and surrounding airflow.
